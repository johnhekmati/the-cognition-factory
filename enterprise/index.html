The problem

Seat-time theater isn’t readiness

Certificates say what someone completed against a standard — not how they think under time constraints, or how they parse unclear requirements and pain-points. Most learning products care about seat-time and scoring modules. They rarely teach people to hold that knowledge against next month’s deliverables, under real pressure.

Teams need practice against your standards, practice that matches what your firm delivers, and scenarios that parallel real engagement work.

This is not a “need more certifications” problem. This is a how do people work under pressure problem.

Learn

One idea until it clicks

Build the map · not binge the playlist

For subjects you need to think through — not just finish.

Not a chatbot tutor. Not a content firehose. Learn is where you build the map: write down what the subject is made of, what always holds true, and how pieces connect — then challenge the fuzzy spots until understanding sticks. Teams keep a living picture of the subject on shared standards. When you are ready to prove it, Practice uses that same map so teaching and testing stay on the same story.

What it does

  • Clear maps

    A picture of the ideas and how they link — structure you can keep and reuse.

  • What always holds

    Core principles that stay true — not just for one lesson.

  • Until you can use it without notes

    Challenge fuzzy explanations until understanding sticks — not until a progress bar fills.

  • Skill that still works next month

    Easy restarts so skill doesn’t quietly fade after a busy week.

Learn builds the map

Then prove it in Practice
Practice

Check what you know — honestly

Closed notes when it counts · not cruise the open book

For goals you need to prove — not just re-read.

Not a comfort scoreboard. Not a random drill dump. Practice shows the gap between recognizing material and being able to use it — then names what went wrong and aims the next run at those gaps until the skill holds. Practice packs are built for a clear goal — an exam, a job skill, a team standard — and always draw from the same map you built in Learn. Same map; harder questions when it counts. One map can feed many packs.

What it does

  • Closed-notes practice

    Test yourself without hints or guessing from how the question looks.

  • Name what went wrong

    Missing idea, wrong method, or “couldn’t put the pieces together” — then fix the right thing.

  • Drills from real gaps

    Practice aimed at what you missed — not random coverage of everything.

  • Track over time

    Skill that still works next month — not one lucky good day.

Then save your place
Save

Pick up where you left off

Clean handoff · not re-scroll the chat

For calendars that interrupt — not perfect study streaks.

Not a chat archive. Not another app to babysit. Save place is how you leave a clean handoff for tomorrow — whether a learner was in Learn, mid Practice, or between cohort sessions. Long sessions break. Apps forget. People get called away. If your notes die when the chat ends, you do not have a system — you have a mood. The loop still holds: a named next step, not a scroll hunt.

What it does

  • Leave a clean stop

    Capture where the cohort was and what still feels fuzzy — enough that restarting is intentional.

  • Name the next step

    Deeper map work, more practice, or a real check — so the next sitting is not a guess.

  • Continuity across calendars

    Cohort and calendar interruptibility — ramps do not die in chat history.

  • Stay oriented

    Quiet layer under save place: where the team is on the map, when open study should become honest practice — not another portal.

When pressure is real — the Dojo
Dojo · Enterprise only

Deliver it when it’s messy

Real scenarios · not another quiz

For skills you need to use — not just pass.

Practice checks what you know. The Dojo puts that skill into messy, timed scenarios — half-finished work, something at stake — so you can see how the call holds under pressure. Structured observation of judgment, not a hiring decision or certification. Same factory as Learn and Practice — the Dojo tests what you actually built. Not the Gym — Gym is the Solo training-wheels floor.

What it does

  • Messy cases on a clock

    Half-finished work, something at stake — pressure that looks like the job, not a quiz timer.

  • Structured observation

    See how the call holds — not a placement guarantee, pass credential, or client-ready stamp.

  • Packs from real work

    Late invoice, three docs that disagree, a dump that must become a plan — flavor of the messy case, not the answer key.

  • Same map underneath

    Scenario packs built from the same map as Learn and Practice — no trick questions from nowhere.

Practice asks “do you know it?” · The Dojo asks “can you work the messy case?”

Partners: graph, patterns, dashboard
Partners

See the map. See the mistakes. Act with eyes open.

Partners are not a logo wall. When maps and practice packs are built from your real work, you get a clear view of how ideas connect — and where people actually stumble.

From your custom courses — not a generic catalog.

Partners can open the knowledge graph behind those courses: how topics link, what holds, where the map branches. That graph can be read as plain charts (including Mermaid diagrams) and as three-dimensional views when a flat list is not enough. You see structure, not a black box.

Wrong answers are not just a score. They feed simple classifiers — for example mechanical slips versus missing ideas versus “could not put the pieces together.” Over time that becomes the dashboard partners actually want: where the cohort is strong, where it is soft, and what to teach or practice next. Honest signals from real attempts — not vanity completion numbers.
